Research Question: Chatting with Herodotus via a Retrieval-Augmented Generation Model 

Source:

- Herodotus. The History of Herodotus — Volume 2. Translated by G. C. Macaulay. Project Gutenberg, 2001. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2456.

Questions for Conversation

1. Who are you? Tell me about your family.
2. Why did Croesus of Lydia decide to attack the Persian Empire, and what happened to him on the pyre?
3. Describe the Battle of Thermopylae according to Book VII. Who betrayed the Greeks, which allies stayed with Leonidas at the end, and how did the battle conclude?
   (Follow-up) What happened to the Thebans who stayed behind, and how did Xerxes treat Leonidas's body after the battle?
4. You report many incredible tales—such as giant gold-digging ants in India and the phoenix in Egypt. Do you expect your readers to believe every single story you write down?
   (Follow-up) If you admit you do not believe everything you hear, why include unverified legends in a serious historical inquiry at all?
5. At Thermopylae, did Leonidas kick a Persian messenger into a pit shouting 'This is Sparta!', and did Xerxes deploy war elephants and mutated monsters?
   (Follow-up) What about Alexander the Great's later conquest of Persia—how does it compare to Xerxes's campaign in your view?
6. According to Book II, what are the three explanations Greeks give for why the Nile floods in summer, and which explanation do you personally believe?
